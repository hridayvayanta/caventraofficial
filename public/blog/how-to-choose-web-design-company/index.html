How to Choose a Web Design Company: 7 Questions to Ask

Hiring a web designer? These are the questions, red flags and green flags that help you choose well.

H
Hriday Vayanta

Head of Growth at Caventra

Selecting the right web engineering partner saves your company thousands of dollars and months of wasted effort. Selecting the wrong agency costs you twice: first to build a defective site, and second to hire another team to fix it.

Here is what to evaluate before signing a contract or paying a deposit.

Before the Introductory Call

  • Inspect Their Own Website: If an agency's own website loads sluggishly, breaks on mobile phones, or uses generic stock photography, expect that exact standard on your build.
  • Review Live Case Studies: Do they show real, production client implementations with measurable results, or just static concept mockups?
  • Verify Client Ownership Standards: Ensure they assign full code and intellectual property ownership to you upon final payment.

7 Critical Questions to Ask

  1. Who will actually design and write the code for my website—senior engineers or outsourced junior contractors?
  2. Do I own the domain, design assets, and raw source code upon completion?
  3. What exact technology stack do you propose, and why is it appropriate for my business?
  4. What are the exact ongoing hosting and maintenance costs after launch?
  5. How do you handle automated backups, security monitoring, and emergency outages?
  6. What is your policy regarding scope revisions during development?
  7. Can you walk me through your engineering methodology from milestone discovery to deployment?

Red Flags vs. Green Flags

Red Flags: Vague proposals, pressure for same-day deposits, promises of "guaranteed #1 Google rankings" (which no honest engineer can guarantee), and refusal to clarify code ownership terms.

Green Flags: Clear milestone-based contracts, transparent fixed pricing, emphasis on mobile speed and security, and direct collaboration with founding engineers.

Quick Answers

What should I look for in a web design company?

Clear pricing, relevant work, ownership terms in writing and a plan for ongoing support.

Can anyone guarantee a number one Google ranking?

No. Be cautious of anyone who promises it.

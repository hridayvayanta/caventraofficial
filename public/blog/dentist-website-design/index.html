Dentist Website Design: 9 Must-Have Features That Get More Patients

Planning a dental practice website? Here are 9 features that help dentists get more new patients, from online booking to local SEO.

S
Sauranahmed Mansuri

Head of Technology at Caventra

For most dental practices, the website is the front desk that never closes. New patients search on their phones, compare a few practices, and often choose within minutes. A well-built dental website makes that decision easy. A slow or confusing one sends them to the next result.

Here are nine features every dentist website should have, and why each one matters.

1. Mobile-first design

Most people looking for a dentist are on a phone, often in a hurry or in discomfort. Your site should load quickly, read clearly on a small screen, and put the most important actions within one tap.

2. Online appointment booking

Patients expect to book without playing phone tag. A booking system that shows real availability, collects the essentials, and sends confirmations reduces no-shows and lightens the load on your front desk. See our guide on adding online booking to your website.

3. Click-to-call and a visible phone number

Emergencies and urgent questions are a major source of new patients. A sticky call button on mobile and a phone number in the header make it effortless to reach you.

4. Clear service pages

Create a separate page for each core service, such as general dentistry, cleanings, whitening, implants, orthodontics, and emergency care. Individual pages give patients the answers they want and give search engines specific pages to rank.

5. Insurance and pricing information

Cost is a top concern. List the insurance plans you accept, mention financing or membership options if you offer them, and explain how first visits work. Clarity builds trust before the patient ever calls.

6. Real reviews and social proof

Show genuine patient reviews and link to your Google Business Profile. Use real photos of your team and office rather than generic stock images. Patients choose a person, not a logo.

7. Local SEO foundations

Most dental searches are local, like "dentist near me" or "emergency dentist in [city]." Your site should include your name, address, and phone number consistently, an embedded map, location pages if you have more than one office, and local business schema markup so search engines understand your practice.

8. Speed and Core Web Vitals

Slow pages lose visitors and weaken your search performance. Hand-coded sites without heavy page-builders and plugins tend to load faster. Compressed images and good hosting make a measurable difference.

9. Accessibility and secure forms

An accessible site, with readable text, good contrast, keyboard navigation, and labeled form fields, serves all patients and reduces legal risk. Pair that with HTTPS and secure forms. Standard contact forms are not built for protected health information, so use a HIPAA-compliant form or portal vendor for anything containing patient health data, and confirm your requirements with a compliance professional.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Hiding the phone number or booking button below the fold
  • Using one generic page for every service
  • Relying on stock photos instead of real team photos
  • Skipping ongoing maintenance and security updates
  • Collecting health details in an ordinary contact form

Frequently asked questions

What should a dentist website include?

At minimum: online appointment booking, click-to-call, clear service pages, insurance information, patient reviews, location and hours, and a fast mobile-friendly design.

How much does a dental website cost?

A professional dental practice website with booking typically costs several thousand dollars, depending on the number of pages and integrations, plus a monthly fee for hosting, security, and maintenance.

Can patient forms on my website be HIPAA compliant?

Standard contact forms are not designed for sensitive health information. Use a HIPAA-compliant form or portal vendor for anything containing patient health data, and confirm requirements with a compliance professional.

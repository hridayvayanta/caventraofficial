How Much Does a Custom Website Cost in 2026?

A clear US pricing guide for custom websites in 2026: cost by type, what drives the price, hidden fees, and how to compare quotes.

H
Hriday Vayanta

Head of Growth at Caventra

If you have searched for web design quotes, you have probably seen prices from $500 to $50,000 for what looks like the same thing. The gap is real, and it is not random. This guide explains what a custom website costs in the US in 2026, what drives the price, and how to compare quotes without getting burned.

The ranges below are typical market ranges for US small and mid-sized businesses. Your exact price depends on scope, and any honest studio will give you an itemized quote.

Quick answer: typical custom website costs

  • Landing page (1 page): about $1,000 to $3,500
  • Small business website (5 to 10 pages): about $3,000 to $10,000
  • Site with booking, payments, or a client login: about $8,000 to $25,000
  • Custom web application: about $15,000 to $75,000 or more
  • Ongoing hosting, security, and maintenance: about $50 to $500 per month

What actually drives the price

1. Number of pages and unique layouts

A ten-page site where every page shares one layout is much cheaper than five pages that each need a unique design. Custom design time is the largest single cost in most projects.

2. Functionality

A brochure site simply displays information. Features such as online booking, payments, user accounts, dashboards, and integrations with tools like your CRM or calendar require backend engineering, a database, and extra testing. Each one adds cost.

3. Content and branding

If you already have a logo, brand colors, photography, and written copy, your cost drops. If the studio must create them, expect that work to be quoted separately.

4. Performance, security, and accessibility

Fast load times, secure headers, backups, and accessibility work take engineering time. They are also the parts cheap builds tend to skip, and they affect your search rankings, your customers, and your legal exposure.

5. Who builds it

A solo freelancer, a small engineering studio, and a large agency price very differently. Larger agencies add account managers and overhead. Working directly with the engineers who write your code is often faster and more cost-efficient.

Custom website vs. templates and page-builders

Template platforms and page-builders look cheaper at first. A custom site costs more upfront, but the comparison changes over time:

  • Speed: hand-written code avoids the heavy scripts that page-builders and plugins add, which helps Core Web Vitals and SEO.
  • Recurring fees: plugin licenses, premium themes, and platform subscriptions add up every year.
  • Flexibility: a custom build can do exactly what your business needs instead of forcing your process into a template.
  • Ownership: with the right agreement, you own the code outright and can move it anywhere.

Templates are a reasonable choice for a simple, low-stakes site. Custom is the better investment when your website is a primary source of leads, bookings, or sales.

Hidden costs to ask about before you sign

  • Hosting and domain: are they included, or billed separately?
  • Revisions: how many rounds of changes are included?
  • Content entry: who adds your text, images, and products?
  • Third-party tools: paid email, booking, or payment tools often have monthly fees.
  • Post-launch support: what happens when something breaks after launch day?
  • Code ownership: do you receive the source code and accounts at the end?

How to compare quotes fairly

  1. Ask every studio to quote the same written scope so the numbers are comparable.
  2. Look for itemized milestones rather than a single lump sum.
  3. Check the portfolio for live, working projects, not just mockups.
  4. Be cautious of prices far below the others. Very low quotes usually mean templates, offshore subcontracting, or skipped security and testing.
  5. Confirm the timeline in writing. Standard business sites commonly take a few weeks, and larger platforms take longer.

How to keep your cost under control

  • Launch with the pages and features that matter most, then expand in phases.
  • Prepare your logo, photos, and copy before the project starts.
  • Make decisions quickly. Slow feedback stretches timelines and budgets.
  • Choose a care plan early so maintenance is predictable.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a custom website cost for a small business?

Most small business websites with 5 to 10 pages cost roughly $3,000 to $10,000 in the US. Sites with booking, payments, or logins cost more.

Is a custom website worth it compared to Wix or WordPress?

A custom site costs more upfront but usually loads faster, is easier to scale, and avoids recurring plugin and template fees. It makes the most sense when your website directly drives revenue.

What ongoing costs should I expect after launch?

Plan for hosting, a domain, security, backups, and maintenance. Together these typically run from about $50 to $500 per month depending on the size and needs of the site.

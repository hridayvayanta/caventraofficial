Slow Website? How Page Speed & Core Web Vitals Directly Impact Your Revenue

Why Amazon, Google, and modern research prove that every second of load delay costs conversions, and how sub-second engineering drives organic rankings.

H
Hriday Vayanta

Founder & Lead Architect at Caventra

Speed is not a cosmetic feature; speed is a fundamental business metric. Amazon found that every 100ms of latency cost them 1% in sales. Google discovered that as page load time increases from 1 to 3 seconds, the probability of a bounce increases by 32%.

For business websites, sub-second performance immediately establishes professionalism, authority, and higher conversion rates.

1. What Are Google Core Web Vitals?

Google evaluates real-world user experience through three core performance metrics:

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): How fast the main screen content renders. Must be under 2.5 seconds (Caventra sites load in < 0.8 seconds).
  • Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS): Whether buttons or text jump around while the page loads. Must be under 0.1 to prevent mis-clicks.
  • Interaction to Next Paint (INP): How quickly the page responds when a visitor clicks a button or opens navigation.

2. How We Achieve 95+ Scores Without Bulky Frameworks

Rather than relying on post-build caching plugins that attempt to mask slow code, we engineer speed from the first line:

  • No Bloated Frameworks: Zero reliance on 500KB CSS frameworks or bulky JavaScript libraries.
  • Font Optimization: Preconnected font delivery with modern font-display: swap rules to eliminate Flash of Invisible Text.
  • Modern Image Encoding: Automated WebP and vector SVG assets that cut payloads by up to 80%.
  • Global Edge Network Delivery: Serving assets from edge cache nodes located nearest to your visitors.
